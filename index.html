How to Use This Toolbox

Start with the learner need, not the label. Use grade-level core instruction first, add targeted support when students need more structure or practice, and intensify intervention when progress data shows the current support is not enough.

Instructional Strategies Across the Three MTSS Tiers

Each tier below includes one practical strategy, how it supports secondary literacy, and how it can connect reading to real-world content.

Tier I · Universal

Explicit Vocabulary Instruction + Real-World Connections

High-quality core instruction for all students.

Targets: academic vocabulary, background knowledge, and access to content-area text.

Why this strategy fits Tier I

Adolescent readers encounter specialized academic language in science, mathematics, social studies, and technical subjects. Explicit vocabulary instruction gives all students a stronger entry point into complex text.

Teacher routine

  1. Select a small number of high-value words that are necessary for understanding the text.
  2. Pronounce each word and provide a student-friendly definition.
  3. Analyze useful roots, prefixes, or suffixes when appropriate.
  4. Show the word in the sentence or passage where students will encounter it.
  5. Connect the word to a visual, demonstration, physical model, or real-world situation.
  6. Require students to use the word in speaking and writing.
  7. Revisit the word across the lesson or unit.

Real-world example

During a science lesson on forces, students examine foam inside a bicycle helmet while learning the word compression. The word is connected to something students can see, touch, and explain before they encounter it in technical text.

How it supports secondary literacy

Students build the background and word knowledge needed to understand grade-level text. The strategy also gives students multiple representations of meaning instead of relying on memorized definitions alone.

Progress evidence

Short vocabulary checks, oral use of terms, exit tickets, and comprehension questions tied directly to the text.

Tier II · Targeted

Collaborative Strategic Reading (CSR)

Supplemental small-group support for students who need more than Tier I alone.

Targets: vocabulary breakdowns and reading comprehension.

CSR sequence

  1. Preview: activate background knowledge and establish a purpose for reading.
  2. Click and Clunk: recognize words or ideas that interrupt comprehension and resolve them systematically.
  3. Get the Gist: identify what the section is mostly about and the most important idea.
  4. Wrap Up: ask questions, review key ideas, and return to the original purpose for reading.

Explicit, systematic instruction

The teacher first models the routine through think-alouds, then moves to guided practice, supported student practice, and increasingly independent use.

Real-world example

Students begin with the question, Why can a bicycle helmet reduce injury even though it cannot prevent a crash? They use CSR to solve unfamiliar academic vocabulary and determine the central idea from a short science passage.

How it supports secondary literacy

CSR gives struggling readers a repeatable process for recognizing when understanding breaks down and for extracting meaning from informational text across content areas.

Progress evidence

Vocabulary accuracy, gist statements, comprehension probes, and repeated performance across comparable texts.

Tier III · Intensive

Multisyllabic Word Reading + Data-Based Individualization

Intensive, individualized support for persistent literacy needs.

Targets: complex word reading, decoding, and access to academic text.

Teacher routine

  1. Use diagnostic information to identify the student's specific word-reading barrier.
  2. Teach recognition of prefixes and suffixes.
  3. Identify the base word or root when possible.
  4. Divide long words into manageable syllables or meaningful parts.
  5. Read each part and blend the complete word.
  6. Determine meaning within the sentence.
  7. Apply the word in connected text.

Data-based individualization

If progress is inadequate, the teacher adjusts the intervention using diagnostic and progress-monitoring data rather than simply repeating the same lesson. Intensity can increase through smaller groups, additional instructional time, more modeling, more opportunities to respond, and more immediate corrective feedback.

Real-world example

A student breaks down photosynthesis, reads the word accurately, connects meaningful word parts to the concept, and then applies the word while reading about how plants convert light energy into stored chemical energy.

How it supports secondary literacy

Efficient word reading frees cognitive resources for comprehension and gives students better access to the complex vocabulary found throughout secondary content areas.

Progress evidence

Frequent word-reading probes, error-pattern analysis, connected-text performance, and response to instructional adaptations.

MTSS Decision Flow

Intervention intensity should respond to student performance.

2. Monitor Learning
3. Add Tier II Support
4. Review Progress Data
5. Intensify / Individualize if Needed
